## Time Series Liquidity
Summary
A liquidity measurement framework that evaluates an institution's ability to access and deploy funds over time, not just in aggregate
Focuses explicitly on the speed and executability of liquidity, rather than static balance sheet totals
Measures liquidity across five ascending time horizons: Minute 1, Day 1, Week 1, Month 1, and Year 1
Each horizon answers a single core question: How much funding can run over this timeframe while the institution remains solvent?
Produces absolute dollar amounts of available liquidity for each time horizon
Translates those amounts into time specific liquidity ratios using a consistent denominator
Uses noncontractual funding as the denominator, ensuring metrics are aligned with funding sources that can leave under stress and actual liquidity risk, not contractual balances
Incorporates both on balance sheet and off balance sheet liquidity sources, including cash and cash equivalents, secured and unsecured borrowing capacity, pledgeable investments and loans, and brokered and listing service deposits
Distinguishes between operational liquidity (subject to policy concentration limits) and contingency liquidity (ignoring policy limits in an emergency)
Replaces traditional scenario based liquidity stress testing by identifying the institution's absolute liquidity breaking point and enabling simple pass/fail assessment against any assumed outflow magnitude
Supports policy setting and governance through board approved minimum ratio limits for each horizon and transparent linkage between liquidity sources, constraints, and outcomes
Designed to be intuitive for management and boards, defensible with regulators, and actionable in real time or rapidly evolving stress events
Definition
Time series liquidity is a liquidity risk measurement framework that assesses an institution's ability to access and deploy cash and cash equivalent funding sources across multiple, explicitly defined time horizons during a stress scenario, while remaining solvent. It emphasizes not just the amount of available liquidity, but the speed at which liquidity can be converted into usable funds, using on and off balance sheet sources such as cash, borrowing capacity, investments, deposits, and pledgeable loans. More specifically, the methodology evaluates liquidity over five ascending horizons—Minute 1, Day 1, Week 1, Month 1, and Year 1—with each horizon representing the maximum estimated cash outflows the institution could withstand over that period. For each time point, a single dollar amount and a corresponding ratio to noncontractual funding are calculated, making time series liquidity a forward looking, executable measure of liquidity resilience rather than a static balance sheet snapshot or a probability based stress test.

## Minute 1 Liquidity
Summary
Measures immediate, intraday liquidity available within one minute
Represents funds the institution can access and deploy instantly
Comprised of Federal Reserve Master Account funds and cash and cash equivalents on balance sheet
Federal Reserve Master Account funds
Cash and cash equivalents on balance sheet
Designed to cover day to day operating cash needs
Establishes the institution's absolute short term solvency floor
Definition
This is the amount of cash that the institution can access and disperse over a one-minute time horizon and remain solvent. Minute 1 liquidity is defined as the amount of Master Account funds, cash, and cash equivalents an institution has on its balance sheet at any given time and, typically, this is the amount required to meet daily cash needs.

## Day 1 Liquidity
Summary
Measures liquidity available over a one day stress horizon
Assumes the onset of a rapid liquidity run
Includes all Minute 1 liquidity and remaining borrowing capacity from FHLB, FRB, and other secured and unsecured borrowing sources
All Minute 1 liquidity
Remaining borrowing capacity from: Federal Home Loan Bank (FHLB), Federal Reserve Bank (FRB), Other secured and unsecured borrowing sources
Indicates how much funding can flee in a single day and still be covered
Considered the most critical operational liquidity metric
Definition
In the event of a liquidity run, this is the amount of cash that the institution can access and disperse over a one-day time horizon and remain solvent. Day 1 liquidity is defined as Minute 1 liquidity plus total remaining borrowing capacity from the Federal Home Loan Bank (FHLB), Federal Reserve Bank (FRB), and other secured and unsecured sources.

## Week 1 Liquidity
Summary
Measures liquidity accessible within a one week contingency timeframe
Includes all Day 1 liquidity, estimated collateral value of unpledged investments that can be sold or pledged, and a portion of brokered and listing service deposit capacity
All Day 1 liquidity
Estimated collateral value of unpledged investments that can be sold or pledged
A portion of brokered and listing service deposit capacity
Typically modeled at 10% each, unless overridden
Reflects liquidity sources that are not instantaneous but executable
Captures the institution's ability to scale liquidity during sustained stress
Definition
In the event of a liquidity run, this is the amount of cash that the institution can access and disperse over a one-week time horizon and remain solvent. Week 1 liquidity is defined as Day 1 liquidity plus the estimated collateral value of unpledged investments (which can be pledged or sold) an institution has on its balance sheet at any given time and the portion of an institution's brokered and listing service deposit capacity (typically, 10% each) that it believes will be able to access within a one-week timeframe in a contingency funding scenario.

## Month 1 Liquidity
Summary
Measures liquidity available over a one month stress period
Includes all Week 1 liquidity and remaining brokered and listing service deposit capacity
All Week 1 liquidity
Remaining brokered and listing service deposit capacity
Assumes full execution of planned market based funding strategies
Represents liquidity available through structured but reliable funding channels
Bridges short term crisis liquidity to more deliberate balance sheet actions
Definition
In the event of a liquidity run, this is the amount of cash that the institution can access and disperse over a one-month time horizon and remain solvent. Month 1 liquidity is defined as Week 1 liquidity plus an institution's remaining brokered and listing service deposit capacity not otherwise included in Week 1 liquidity.

## Year 1 Liquidity
Summary
Measures liquidity accessible over a one year stress horizon
Includes all Month 1 liquidity and unpledged but pledgeable loan capacity
All Month 1 liquidity
Unpledged but pledgeable loan capacity
Captures the institution's maximum liquidity backstop
Defines the absolute breaking point before insolvency
Used to assess long run balance sheet survivability under stress
Definition
In the event of a liquidity run, this is the amount of cash that the institution can access and disperse over a one-year time horizon and remain solvent. Year 1 liquidity is defined as Month 1 liquidity plus the amount of unpledged but pledgeable loans an institution has on its balance sheet at any given time.
